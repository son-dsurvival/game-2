# 🌍 EARTH UPGRADE WORLD FRAME

## Canon Scope

This document establishes:

- Earth immediately before the Upgrade
- the opening event
- immutable cosmological and System laws
- the sources of future peoples and powers
- the boundaries within which the world may develop

The game begins on **Day Zero**.

Future history is not predetermined. Nations, guilds, religions, species, and other powers rise or fall dynamically according to play and the state of the world.

Exact resolution formulas, XP calculations, combat rules, and noncombat rules remain governed by `game rules (1).md`.

---

## The World Before

Earth begins as ordinary modern Earth.

Before Day Zero, humanity has:

- no confirmed magic
- no public System
- no recognized classes
- no measurable mana
- no Monster Dungeons
- no verified knowledge of Tier-6 or Tier-7 existence

Modern governments, militaries, corporations, infrastructure, communications, and economies exist normally until the Upgrade begins.

Humanity is given no preparation period.

---

## The Decree

At the same instant, every human receives and understands the following message regardless of language:

> `[World Designation: Earth]`  
> `[By authority of Tier-7 Law, compulsory World Upgrade has been authorized.]`  
> `[All compatible life will now enter the Evolution System.]`  
> `[Upgrade begins now.]`

The message is a System notice citing Tier-7 authority. It is not confirmed direct speech from the Tier-7 existence.

No tutorial or survival warning precedes activation.

Earth was not selected because humanity succeeded, failed, matured, or satisfied a known condition.

The decree is arbitrary.

No discoverable moral justification is provided.

---

## The Opening

The Upgrade begins immediately after the notice.

At once:

- humanity receives conscious System access
- all compatible Earth life enters the Evolution System
- mana becomes part of Earth's reality
- the first Monster Dungeons manifest
- monsters begin entering or appearing within the world
- real locations begin transforming into dungeon regions
- portals and spatial breaches open
- existing ecosystems begin adapting

There is no protected global tutorial stage.

Modern civilization enters the crisis with its existing knowledge, equipment, institutions, and divisions.

---

## The Evolution System

### Core Nature

The System is an **evolution engine**.

It is not moral.

It does not exist to reward goodness, punish evil, preserve humanity, or guarantee fairness.

It recognizes:

- struggle
- survival
- adaptation
- discovery
- achievement
- mastery
- meaningful risk
- consequential action

Its governing principle is:

> **Pressure produces adaptation. Adaptation produces evolution.**

The System itself seeks no known outcome beyond continued growth and escalation.

Tier-6 beings may observe, favor, exploit, or enjoy the process, but entertainment is not the System's governing law.

### Meaningful Action

Growth cannot be farmed through empty repetition.

An action may produce advancement when it presents meaningful difficulty or advances life, survival, development, or mastery.

If a challenge falls below the minimum relevance threshold defined in `game rules (1).md`, it grants no XP.

This applies to combat and noncombat growth.

A fighter, healer, engineer, explorer, craftsperson, organizer, researcher, or builder may all evolve through genuinely consequential action.

### Universal Integration

The System applies to all compatible Earth life.

This includes:

- humans
- animals
- plants
- fungi
- microorganisms where System relevance exists
- later artificial or constructed beings recognized by the System

System access does not imply equal awareness.

Humans receive conscious interfaces. Other life may initially experience the System through instinct, mutation, adaptation, collective behavior, or other species-appropriate forms.

## Growth and Classes

### Dual XP System

Growth follows the two paths established in `game rules (1).md`.

#### Normal XP

Normal XP represents broad evolution through:

- survival
- hardship
- exploration
- training
- discovery
- meaningful combat
- difficult crafting
- consequential problem-solving
- major achievement

Normal Levels grant general statistical growth.

#### Class XP

Class XP represents mastery of an established role.

It is gained only through purposeful, class-aligned actions that deepen or transform that role.

Class Levels grant:

- skills
- spells
- stances
- forms
- passive features
- transformations
- specialized abilities

Normal XP and Class XP are separate and cannot be exchanged.

### Classless Beginning

Every human begins the Upgrade **classless**.

No class is granted solely because of a former job, claimed identity, personality, or desire.

Meaningful action unlocks class possibilities.

Examples:

- treating the injured under genuine danger may unlock a healing path
- organizing a functioning shelter may unlock a leadership path
- defending others in combat may unlock a martial or guardian path
- restoring damaged infrastructure may unlock an engineering path

Past experience may affect capability, but demonstrated action determines System recognition.

### One Evolving Class

Each being may possess only **one active class**.

That class may:

- branch
- specialize
- deepen
- transform
- develop prestige paths
- acquire new expressions through breakthroughs

Unrelated classes cannot be collected casually.

An individual's class becomes a record of what that being repeatedly proves itself capable of becoming.

### Optional Quests

The System may offer optional quests when it recognizes meaningful opportunities.

Quest rewards may include:

- XP
- Class XP
- System currency
- class development
- titles
- access to System functions
- other context-appropriate rewards

Rejecting a quest causes no automatic punishment.

The unresolved situation may still produce natural consequences.

## Canonical Tier Structure

Tiers are qualitative power brackets, not merely level ranges.

Levels increase capacity within a bracket. A Tier breakthrough changes the kind of influence a being can exert.

The social titles below coexist with the mechanical definitions in `game rules (1).md`.

### Tier 0 — Common Rank

The starting bracket for ordinary humans and newly integrated life.

It includes civilians, trained professionals, soldiers, apprentices, basic hunters, and early System users.

Tier-0 capability remains within broadly human-scale limits.

### Tier 1 — Skilled Rank

The beginning of sustained superhuman capability.

Veterans, established specialists, evolved predators, trained System users, and proven class holders occupy this bracket.

They can dominate ordinary local encounters.

### Tier 2 — Elite Rank

A bracket capable of controlling settlements, small battlefields, and significant territory.

At this tier, environmental impact and civilization-level evolutionary possibilities begin to emerge.

### Tier 3 — High Power Rank

A city-level strategic bracket.

Tier-3 beings become political and military assets whose actions can reshape major population centers and regional conflicts.

### Tier 4 — Imperial Rank

A country-level strategic bracket.

Tier-4 beings can influence nations, ecosystems, and large military theaters.

### Tier 5 — Sovereign Rank

The highest world-bound bracket.

Tier-5 beings can alter continental balances and become powers comparable to states or civilization-scale threats.

### Tier-6 — Ascension

Tier 6 is not a sustainable mortal bracket inside the world.

A Tier-5 being that successfully creates and stabilizes a Tier-6 skill undergoes Ascension.

The being:

- transcends the mortal System
- leaves the physical world
- becomes a nascent Tier-6 entity
- can no longer enter Earth directly
- may influence Earth only through permitted indirect methods

There are no resident, world-bound Tier-6 mortals.

### Breakthrough Law

Levels alone cannot advance a being into a higher tier.

A new tier requires the successful creation of a skill belonging to that tier.

Breakthrough therefore depends on:

- experimentation
- knowledge
- creativity
- execution
- risk
- a genuine new category of capability

Grinding increases capacity. Innovation changes tiers.

## Mana and Technology

The Upgrade does not automatically disable modern technology.

Existing technology remains functional while its infrastructure, fuel, maintenance, personnel, and communications survive.

Conventional technology begins at Basic or Tier 0 capability.

This means:

- firearms remain dangerous during the opening
- military vehicles and explosives matter
- communications remain strategically vital
- modern medicine and engineering retain value

However, ordinary technology cannot scale indefinitely against higher-tier beings and environments.

Technology may evolve through System-recognized engineering using:

- dungeon substances
- monster components
- mana-active materials
- evolved manufacturing methods
- class skills
- System research

Possible developments include:

- mana-integrated firearms
- evolved vehicles
- fortified System bases
- magical power grids
- enhanced drones
- adaptive armor
- artificial life
- System-recognized networks

Magic does not replace technology.

Both become evolutionary paths, and their integration may create entirely new ones.

## Monster Dungeons

### Core Definition

A Monster Dungeon is a persistent origin region that introduces, produces, transforms, or sustains hostile life and elevated mana conditions.

"Dungeon" is a System classification, not a structural description.

On upgraded Earth, a dungeon may appear as:

- a portal into a separate space
- a transformed skyscraper
- a corrupted hospital
- an altered subway network
- a stadium or shopping district
- a crater
- an overgrown forest
- a flooded region
- a military facility
- a spatially impossible interior
- another persistent anomaly

### Mixed Manifestation

Some dungeons exist inside altered Earth locations.

Others are portals or breaches leading into spaces that do not obey ordinary geography.

No universal dungeon shape exists.

### Unknown Origin

The true origin of dungeon creatures is unresolved.

Evidence is contradictory:

- some monsters appear artificially generated
- some possess memories or cultures that seemingly predate Earth's Upgrade
- some resemble transformed Earth life
- some dungeons contain ruins implying lost civilizations
- some environments behave like fragments of another world

No theory is confirmed canon.

The System does not explain the contradiction.

### Progressive Activation

Earth's dungeon network does not activate at full global strength on Day Zero.

The first Tier-0 dungeons manifest immediately, but additional dungeons and higher-tier regions emerge through later World Stages.

The exact triggers governing World Stage advancement remain unresolved.

Once a dungeon manifests, its tier ceiling is fixed.

### Fixed Tier Ceiling

A dungeon cannot initially produce an entity or substance above its assigned tier.

Examples:

- a Tier-0 Dungeon produces up to Tier 0
- a Tier-3 Dungeon produces up to Tier 3
- a Tier-5 Dungeon produces up to Tier 5

The ceiling governs dungeon production, not the permanent potential of what is produced. Once spawned, a creature becomes an independent System participant and may advance beyond its originating dungeon through XP, skill creation, and tier breakthroughs.

Higher-tier dungeons are rarer and generally produce fewer but far more dangerous ceiling-tier entities.

### Production Budget

Every active dungeon generates a renewable production budget.

It may spend that budget on:

- large numbers of lower-tier creatures
- balanced mixed-tier populations
- specialized creatures
- a small number of ceiling-tier creatures
- saving pressure for elites, bosses, or a future surge

A dungeon's nature influences how it allocates its budget. Two dungeons of the same tier may therefore produce radically different populations.

Unused production may be stored as dungeon pressure. Suppression can reduce active output or destroy creatures as they emerge, but unresolved stored pressure may contribute to a later breach or Monster Tide.

### Baseline Spawn Rates

The following bands describe approximate maximum normal **ceiling-tier output** when a dungeon spends most of its available budget at its own tier:

| Dungeon Tier | Approximate Ceiling-Tier Output |
| --- | --- |
| Tier 0 | Hundreds to thousands daily |
| Tier 1 | Dozens to hundreds daily |
| Tier 2 | Several to dozens weekly |
| Tier 3 | Several weekly |
| Tier 4 | Several monthly |
| Tier 5 | Approximately one yearly |

A dungeon that spends its budget on lower-tier creatures may produce a much greater total headcount. A high-tier dungeon can therefore support a large lower-tier ecosystem while generating its own ceiling-tier creatures far less often.

Actual output is modified by:

- suppression strength
- stored dungeon pressure
- ambient mana
- available territory
- surrounding ecology
- World Stage conditions
- dungeon damage or instability
- dynamic events

Output states range from **Suppressed** through **Stable**, **Strained**, and **Critical**. A Monster Tide represents a breach in which accumulated output and independent populations exceed local control.

### Individual Creature Growth

Dungeon creatures follow the same universal growth laws as other System participants.

They use:

- Normal XP for broad evolution
- Class XP after earning a class
- the same meaningful-action threshold
- the same anti-farming rules
- the same skill-creation requirement for tier breakthroughs

There is no hidden universal monster XP bonus.

A creature may grow faster because of an explicit factor such as:

- a species trait
- predation or core consumption
- inherited adaptations
- high dungeon mana
- constant meaningful combat
- blessings
- mutation
- corruption
- favorable environmental conditions

Growth has no guaranteed calendar rate. A creature surrounded by trivial prey may stagnate, while one surviving repeated meaningful threats may advance rapidly.

### Independent Population Growth

Dungeon spawning and natural reproduction are separate sources of population.

A species capable of reproduction receives one general reproduction band:

| Reproduction Band | Typical Potential Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Swarm | Population may double within hours or days |
| Rapid | Population may double within days or weeks |
| Standard | Population may double over months |
| Slow | Population growth requires years |
| Singular | The species cannot reproduce naturally |

These are potential rates, not guarantees. Food, territory, mana, predation, disease, internal conflict, and organized suppression affect actual growth.

Escaped populations may develop through the following stages:

1. **Spawn Group** — mobile survivors without permanent territory.
2. **Nest** — secures shelter, food, and a reproduction site.
3. **Colony** — sustains reproduction and develops specialized roles.
4. **Domain** — controls territory, subordinate nests, and resource routes under powerful leaders.
5. **Polity** — a sapient population develops laws, diplomacy, institutions, and an economy.

Destroying an independent nest or colony does not reduce the dungeon's permanent production capability. Suppressing the dungeon does not automatically destroy populations that have already become self-sustaining.

### Closure Law

No known world-bound being can permanently close a Monster Dungeon.

A dungeon may be:

- suppressed
- contained
- delayed
- monitored
- exploited
- temporarily sealed
- reduced in output

It cannot be permanently erased under known laws.

This remains true even for System-purchased safe-zone effects.

### Renewable Resources

Dungeons produce monsters and elevated concentrations of useful substances.

Possible resources include:

- mana-active metals
- herbs
- reagents
- catalysts
- monster components
- construction materials
- energy-bearing cores
- anomalous liquids or gases

Resource quality cannot exceed the dungeon's tier ceiling.

Output recovers over time, making dungeons renewable strategic assets rather than ordinary mines.

The most valuable resources generally exist closest to the dungeon origin, where danger is greatest.

### Monster Tides

A Monster Tide occurs when dungeon output and surrounding hostile populations exceed local suppression capacity.

Possible causes include:

- neglect
- infrastructure collapse
- war
- failed suppression
- ecological disruption
- elite monster emergence
- linked dungeon activity
- deliberate interference

A Tide is pressure overflow, not merely a random horde.

## Monster Classification

"Monster" is not a permanent species label.

It is a contextual System classification associated with active hostility.

Any being may temporarily qualify, including:

- humans
- Dungeonborn
- evolved animals
- constructs
- divergent humans
- other sapient beings

The classification reflects an active threat toward protected life, territory, or objectives.

It does not determine moral worth.

A peaceful Dungeonborn being is not automatically a monster. A human actively attacking a protected settlement may be classified as one.

This rule allows No-Monster Zones to function without becoming racial barriers.

## The Five Great Peoples

The Five Great Peoples are broad origin categories rather than unified political blocs.

### 1. Baseline Humanity

Humans who retain their original species identity despite System growth.

They remain culturally and politically divided.

### 2. Divergent Humanity

Humans transformed through:

- class evolution
- mana adaptation
- mutation
- undeath
- technological integration
- blessings
- corruption
- deliberate biological change

Divergent humans may remain socially human even when no longer biologically baseline.

### 3. Awakened Earthborn

Earth-native nonhuman life recognized as civilization-capable by the System.

Tier 2 is the minimum threshold for this recognition.

Reaching Tier 2 does not guarantee sapience or civilization.

The System judges each candidate according to its development, adaptation, experience, and evolutionary direction.

Some Tier-2 creatures remain powerful beasts. Rare individuals may become founders of new peoples.

### 4. Dungeonborn

Sapient beings first encountered within or through Monster Dungeons.

The label describes where Earth encountered them, not their true origin or moral nature.

Dungeonborn peoples may be invaders, refugees, traders, explorers, prisoners, or survivors of histories unknown to Earth.

### 5. Constructed and Anomalous Life

Beings that fit no stable biological lineage, including possible:

- awakened artificial intelligences
- golems
- artificial organisms
- collective minds
- living machines
- system anomalies
- hybrid entities

Recognition as a person depends on System judgment and demonstrated autonomy, not the material from which a being was made.

## System Currency

### Nature

The System introduces a universal digital currency recorded through System status.

It cannot be conventionally counterfeited and may be transferred through authorized System functions.

The System does not sell generated portable weapons, food, medicine, materials, or finished advancement goods.

All ordinary physical goods must still be:

- produced
- hunted
- harvested
- crafted
- recovered
- traded

The System has two narrow exceptions:

1. It may construct integrated **Base infrastructure** that cannot be removed, converted into ordinary inventory, or resold as separate goods.
2. It may sell licensed **knowledge and skill instruction**, which must still be studied, practiced, and mastered.

The currency derives value from access to trusted trade infrastructure, Base development, learnable knowledge, and other System services.

### Currency Issuance

New currency may enter circulation through all of the following:

- optional quests
- meaningful achievements
- first discoveries
- breakthroughs
- dungeon suppression
- rescues and public defense
- qualifying monster victories and bounties
- surrendering monster cores or approved materials to the System

Anti-farming rules still apply.

Trivial or repetitive activity eventually produces no reward. Resources surrendered for exchange are permanently consumed.

### System Services

Currency may purchase services such as:

- marketplace listings
- escrow
- binding contracts
- verified ownership
- item identification and appraisal
- knowledge and skill-manual licenses
- secure transfers
- storage functions
- communication functions
- guild and party registration
- base registration
- temporary No-Monster Zones
- System-mediated Tier-6 contact

These payments remove currency from circulation and act as economic sinks.

## System Knowledge and Skill Market

### Core Law

The System sells **instruction, not mastery**.

Purchasing knowledge does not place information directly into a buyer's mind. It grants access to material that must be read, understood, practiced, tested, and integrated like an exceptionally well-structured book, manual, blueprint, or course.

Buying a skill manual does not grant the skill. Buying a spellbook does not teach the spell. Buying an engineering design does not create the machine.

### Complete Catalog

The full catalog of System-approved, non-Tier-6 learning material is visible from Day Zero.

Any person may view catalog entries and purchase any listed material they can afford, regardless of their current tier or class.

Visibility does not provide comprehension. A Tier-0 learner may purchase Tier-5 material, but may lack the language, foundational theory, stats, facilities, materials, safety, and lower-tier skills needed to understand or apply it.

"Complete catalog" means every purchasable System curriculum is listed. It does not mean the catalog is omniscient. It does not automatically reveal:

- private memories
- future events
- undiscovered identities
- the true origin of every dungeon
- Tier-6 secrets
- the motives or nature of the Tier-7 existence

Tier-6 knowledge is not sold through the ordinary catalog.

### Types of Purchasable Instruction

The catalog may contain:

- academic and professional knowledge
- combat methods
- spell theory and spellbooks
- skill-development manuals
- crafting recipes
- alchemical formulas
- engineering blueprints
- medical procedures
- cultivation methods
- monster and dungeon studies
- class theory
- languages
- research methods
- Base operation and facility training

A catalog purchase supplies structured instruction appropriate to its subject, potentially including:

- explanations
- diagrams
- exercises
- worked examples
- practice sequences
- prerequisite maps
- safety warnings
- evaluation standards

### Complexity and Power

Learning complexity rises with the tier and potential impact of the material.

Higher-tier instruction generally has:

- exponentially higher prices
- more prerequisites
- longer study requirements
- harder comprehension tasks
- greater practice demands
- higher facility and material requirements
- more dangerous failure consequences

More complex learning can support more powerful results, but ownership alone grants no power.

A high-tier book may be readable at a basic linguistic level while remaining functionally unusable without years of prerequisite study.

### Learning Procedure

Learning from purchased material follows the normal time and noncombat rules in `game rules (1).md`.

A learner must:

1. acquire valid access
2. satisfy or study the prerequisites
3. spend the required Phase Time reading and practicing
4. resolve meaningful comprehension, experimentation, or training tasks
5. demonstrate the capability under valid conditions
6. complete any required skill-creation or tier-breakthrough process

Reading alone does not automatically create a modifier, skill, spell, class level, or tier advancement.

Normal XP may be awarded when the study presents meaningful growth. Class XP applies only when the learning genuinely deepens the learner's active class.

### License Types

Knowledge is sold under several access licenses:

| License | Authorized Access |
| --- | --- |
| Personal | One designated learner |
| Party | Registered members of one party |
| Base | Authorized residents or users of one Base |
| Guild | Registered members of one guild |
| Institutional | A large school, government, public program, or comparable organization |

Broader licenses cost substantially more.

A learner may naturally remember, explain, and teach what they genuinely understand. The System does not erase learned knowledge or prevent ordinary teaching.

However, notes or secondhand lessons do not reproduce the original System curriculum's organized interface, validation, exercises, diagrams, updates, or progress tracking. Copying the displayed material does not create another licensed System copy.

### Relationship to Bases

A Library, Archive, Training Hall, Laboratory, or similar Base room can host compatible licenses and improve their use.

Depending on its type and tier, such a room may provide:

- multi-user access
- study modifiers
- protected storage
- practice environments
- instructor support tools
- progress records
- research integration
- reduced duplication between related subjects

The room itself does not include every book or skill. Knowledge licenses must be purchased, discovered, donated, or created separately.

This makes knowledge-focused Bases, universities, guilds, and governments strategically important without turning purchased information into instant power.

## Bases and Temporary Safe Zones

### Core Definition

A Base is a high-cost System gift purchased with System currency and anchored to a building or controlled site.

It is more than registered property. Conversion turns the chosen structure into an expandable System facility capable of supporting specialized rooms, services, and upgrades that would otherwise require far more construction time, expertise, labor, and coordination.

A Base exists primarily to purchase **convenience, stability, and infrastructure**.

It does not automatically provide:

- raw materials
- consumable supplies
- recipes
- knowledge
- skilled workers
- finished products
- permanent safety

### Conversion

A person or recognized group must control a suitable building or site and pay the conversion price.

The System then:

- repairs or stabilizes enough of the structure to support conversion
- establishes the Base boundary
- creates a Base interface
- records ownership and authorized access
- assigns the Base its initial tier
- assigns one inherent Base Trait
- opens room and expansion slots

Base fixtures created by the System are integrated into the facility. They cannot be removed and sold as ordinary equipment.

### Inherent Base Trait

Every Base receives a trait derived from the original purpose, structure, and history of the converted building.

Examples include:

| Original Building | Possible Base Trait |
| --- | --- |
| Hospital or clinic | Medical Infrastructure |
| School or university | Accelerated Study |
| Library or archive | Knowledge Preservation |
| Police station | Threat Detection |
| Military installation | Fortification |
| Factory | Mass Production |
| Farm or greenhouse | Cultivation |
| Hotel or apartment complex | Population Capacity |
| Data center | System Processing |
| Religious building | Tier-6 Communion |

The trait does not perform work by itself. It improves relevant rooms, actions, capacity, or System services.

The Base Trait rises when the Base advances in tier.

### Room System

Rooms are specialized System modules purchased and constructed inside the Base.

Possible rooms include:

- alchemy laboratories
- blacksmith forges
- gardens and greenhouses
- libraries and archives
- workshops
- kitchens
- infirmaries
- training halls
- storage vaults
- communication rooms
- command centers
- defensive rooms
- monster-processing facilities
- research laboratories
- Tier-6 communion chambers

A room provides the integrated facility, tools, controlled environment, and applicable facility modifier for its documented purpose.

It does not create materials, workers, mastery, or automatic products. For example:

- an alchemy room still requires ingredients, recipes, and an alchemist
- a forge still requires metal and a capable smith
- a garden still requires seeds, cultivation, and growth time
- a library stores, protects, organizes, and assists access to knowledge but does not contain all knowledge automatically

Room bonuses follow the facility and resolution rules in `game rules (1).md`.

### Construction and Expediting

Purchased rooms and upgrades require construction time.

Time increases sharply with:

- room tier
- room size
- technical complexity
- magical complexity
- the amount of expansion required
- damage to the Base

Additional currency may be spent to expedite construction.

Expediting purchases System labor and accelerated integration. It does not waive room prerequisites, tier requirements, or required physical inputs explicitly listed by the upgrade.

This allows wealthy Bases to exchange large amounts of currency for time and convenience without receiving free finished goods.

### Room and Base Tiers

Every Base and specialized room has a tier.

Rules:

- a new Base begins at Tier 0 unless a separate effect explicitly states otherwise
- a room cannot normally exceed its Base's current tier
- advancing a Base requires specified core and specialization rooms to reach the required tier
- the upgrade also requires a major currency payment and any stated structural conditions
- unmet room requirements cannot be bypassed by paying an expediting fee

Advancing the Base may improve:

- its inherent trait
- its room cap
- its maximum room tier
- its recognized territory
- its population capacity
- available System services
- defensive and utility options
- expansion methods

A Base must therefore develop as a functioning system rather than upgrading one favored room while neglecting everything else.

### Tiered Expansion

Base expansion changes with tier.

- **Early tiers** use the existing building footprint and ordinary internal reconstruction.
- **Middle tiers** unlock System-assisted physical rebuilding, reinforcement, and outward expansion across controlled land.
- **High tiers** may unlock spatial compression or extradimensional interior expansion while retaining a fixed external anchor.

Spatial expansion increases usable interior space. It does not automatically increase claimed exterior territory or remove the need to defend the Base's physical anchor.

### Why Bases Are Valuable

Bases provide concentrated advantages that ordinary buildings cannot match:

- rapid access to specialized facilities
- reliable facility modifiers
- organized storage and records
- protected knowledge and infrastructure
- marketplace and escrow access
- guild, party, and population administration
- communication systems
- controlled crafting and research environments
- room-based production chains
- faster rebuilding after disasters
- access to temporary protection services
- a valid anchor for Tier-6 communication
- a scalable center for settlements, factions, and economies

A Base does not replace the surrounding settlement. It acts as the settlement's System-supported core.

The Base's main advantage is not free power. It is the ability to spend very large quantities of currency to replace years of construction and coordination with reliable System infrastructure.

### No-Monster Zones

A No-Monster Zone is a temporary hostility-suppression service centered on a recognized Base or other qualified location.

It does not permanently destroy monsters or close dungeons.

Its duration and capability depend on the purchased service and applicable System limits.

Because Monster is a hostility classification, the field is not restricted by species.

A No-Monster Zone is a paid temporary perk, not an automatic permanent feature of every Base.

### Tier-6 Contact

A qualified Base with the required room or System function may spend currency to open a mediated channel toward Tier-6 entities.

Payment only opens the channel.

It does not:

- compel an answer
- force a blessing
- guarantee truthful communication
- create a contract automatically
- permit direct physical entry

## System Civilization Hierarchy

### Civilization Law

Bases are the structural seeds from which System-recognized civilizations grow.

An isolated Base can preserve a group. A network of subordinate Bases can support a settlement. Nested networks allow settlements to become states and states to become empires.

This hierarchy is available to all recognized peoples. It is the primary System mechanism through which humans, Divergent Humans, Awakened Earthborn, Dungeonborn, and Constructed or Anomalous Life can create durable civilizations under permanent dungeon pressure.

Political names do not control System rank. A Tier-4 civilization is classified as a Kingdom even if it is governed as a republic, council, hive, corporate state, or machine collective.

### Civilization Ranks

| Tier | System Civilization Form | Required Core Base | Minimum Leader Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 0 | Independent Base / Outpost | Tier 0 | Tier 0 |
| Tier 1 | Village | Tier 1 | Tier 1 |
| Tier 2 | Town | Tier 2 | Tier 2 |
| Tier 3 | City | Tier 3 | Tier 3 |
| Tier 4 | Kingdom | Tier 4 | Tier 4 |
| Tier 5 | Empire | Tier 5 | Tier 5 |

Tier 6 has no world-bound civilization form. Tier-6 advancement is Ascension out of the physical world.

The minimum leader may be an individual ruler or a System-recognized governing authority containing a qualifying leader. Possessing a high-tier leader alone is not enough; the entire infrastructure pyramid must also exist.

### Five-Branch Pyramid

Every civilization above Tier 0 requires five direct subordinate cores from the tier immediately below it.

Those subordinate cores bring their own complete lower-tier networks into the hierarchy.

| Upgrade | Required Subordinate Network |
| --- | --- |
| Village | 5 Tier-0 Bases |
| Town | 5 Villages and 25 Tier-0 Bases |
| City | 5 Towns, 25 Villages, and 125 Tier-0 Bases |
| Kingdom | 5 Cities, 25 Towns, 125 Villages, and 625 Tier-0 Bases |
| Empire | 5 Kingdoms, 25 Cities, 125 Towns, 625 Villages, and 3,125 Tier-0 Bases |

The central Core Base is additional to the subordinate network shown in the table.

Ordinary homes, farms, workshops, shelters, and unregistered settlements may surround each Base, but they do not count as subordinate Bases until converted and recognized by the System.

### Upgrade Requirements

A civilization upgrade requires all of the following:

- a central Core Base at the required tier
- a recognized leader or governing authority of the required tier
- the complete Five-Branch subordinate pyramid
- required Core rooms and infrastructure
- sufficient population and resource stability
- stable communication and administrative links
- a major System-currency payment
- completion of the civilization-upgrade process

Currency can expedite eligible construction and integration time. It cannot waive the tier, infrastructure, leadership, or subordinate-network requirements.

### Counting and Allegiance

A subordinate Core can count toward only one superior civilization hierarchy at a time.

Alliances, trade agreements, defensive pacts, proximity, and claimed borders do not create subservience by themselves.

System-recognized subservience may be established through:

#### Voluntary Treaty

The subordinate controller accepts an allegiance contract and joins the superior hierarchy under negotiated terms.

#### Accepted Surrender

The losing controller accepts transfer or vassal terms after conflict.

#### Completed Conquest

A conqueror may force integration by:

- seizing the subordinate Base Core
- defeating or removing organized control
- maintaining effective occupation
- paying the System integration cost
- completing the conquest-recognition process

A declaration or temporary raid is not enough.

Conquest grants recognized control, not loyalty or mind control. Residents and former controllers may resist, sabotage, escape, or organize rebellion.

### Network Benefits

Civilization rank unlocks coordination that isolated Bases cannot reproduce efficiently.

Possible benefits include:

- linked administration and population records
- hierarchy-wide communication
- coordinated threat warnings
- trade and logistics integration
- shared knowledge-license administration
- organized dungeon-suppression regions
- large-scale construction projects
- inter-Base production chains
- coordinated temporary safe zones
- civilization-scale quests and services
- recognized borders and vassal relations
- improved recovery after regional disasters

These functions create the infrastructure needed for a people to survive repeated Monster Tides and preserve knowledge across generations.

### Cohesion Crisis and Rank Loss

If a civilization loses enough qualifying subordinate Bases, leadership, infrastructure, or stability to fall below its rank requirement, it enters a **Cohesion Crisis**.

The crisis has two stages:

1. **Immediate degradation** — rank-dependent services, capacity, coordination, and bonuses are reduced or suspended according to the deficit.
2. **Restoration period** — the civilization receives limited time to restore the missing requirements, replace destroyed subordinates, or negotiate new allegiance.

If the requirements are restored before the period ends, full recognition returns.

If they are not restored, the civilization permanently downgrades to the highest rank it can still support. This may trigger cascading crises in superior civilizations that depended on it.

A rebellion therefore threatens more than territory. The loss of one Core can destabilize every higher layer of the hierarchy.

### Civilizational Function

The hierarchy does not make civilization peaceful, fair, or permanent.

It makes civilization structurally possible.

Bases preserve specialized production and knowledge. Villages preserve local populations. Towns coordinate several communities. Cities support regional systems. Kingdoms organize large territories. Empires connect multiple civilization-scale powers against world-level pressure.

This is how the peoples of an upgraded world survive long enough to become civilizations rather than scattered survivors.

## Cosmology

### Tier-7 — Unknown Absolute

The Tier-7 existence is singular.

Known facts:

- Tier-7 authority supersedes the Evolution System
- Earth was upgraded under Tier-7 Law
- no limit has been observed
- no confirmed direct physical interaction has occurred
- no known being can compel or meaningfully oppose it

Unknown facts:

- why Earth was chosen
- whether the Tier-7 existence created Earth
- whether it observes the Upgrade
- whether it possesses motives comprehensible to lesser beings

The System notice proves authority, not intent.

### Tier-6 Entities

Multiple Tier-6 entities exist outside the world.

They:

- cannot physically enter Earth
- are not equal to one another
- possess unknown hierarchies and agendas
- may observe the upgraded world
- may influence events indirectly

Possible methods include:

- blessings
- followers
- champions
- artifacts
- dreams
- mediated communication
- distortions
- anomalies
- indirect events

### Followers and Champions

A Tier-6 entity may maintain many followers.

Followers may receive:

- communication
- minor blessings
- optional quests
- artifacts
- temporary influence

Each Tier-6 entity may maintain only **one true champion** at a time.

A champion is its singular world-bound representative, not an incarnation through which the entity may enter Earth.

All granted power remains subject to System constraints.

### Ascended Beings

A mortal that completes Tier-6 Ascension joins the external layer.

It becomes subject to the same prohibition against direct re-entry.

Ascension therefore grants transcendence at the cost of permanent physical separation from the world.
